    Incoherent fleet · all blast. Three phones, three Soundcore 2s — each iPhone pairs 1:1 to its own speaker and transmits at once. Hop schedules are independent per tab (Math.random locally; no shared seed or sync). Listen is optional loopback/debug only.
    Soundcore 2 ultrasonic warning. Anker lists 12 W dual neodymium drivers, BassUp + DSP, IPX7, 5 200 mAh / 24 h, Bluetooth 5.0 — no frequency-response curve published. BT codecs (SBC/AAC) and speaker DSP will likely crush or erase 17–23 kHz carriers. See SPEC.md.
    iPhone / Safari. Add to Home Screen for a full-screen app. Audio and mic only unlock after you tap Signal / Transmit / Listen. Use volume buttons — silent switch does not mute Web Audio on modern iOS, but hardware volume still caps the carrier. Prefer HTTPS (this Vercel deploy).
    Over-air needs the browser's cleanup turned off. Echo cancellation, noise suppression and auto gain control all assume they are handling speech, and all three will destroy a 20 kHz carrier — the capture request explicitly disables them. Microphone access also needs a secure context (HTTPS), which this deploy provides.
    Both ends must run at 48 kHz to reach the top of the band. A capture device at 44.1 kHz tops out at 22 050 Hz, and many laptop mics roll off hard above 18 kHz regardless of sample rate. If the decode fails, narrow the band downward before assuming the link is broken — the green input trace on the spectrum plot shows exactly how much of the band survived the trip. Raising symbol duration also buys margin, which is why the default moved to 200 ms; over-air is far less forgiving than loopback.
    The phrase is carried as tones, not as speech. Speech identity lives in formant ratios spanning roughly 100 Hz to 8 kHz — an 80:1 range. 17 500 to 21 000 Hz is a 1.2:1 range, so no transposition of a voice survives the move. Every character gets its own frequency instead, and the receiver quantises each detected tone back to the nearest slot in that plan.
